        How these numbers work
        • Data: Open-Meteo Air Quality API (CC BY 4.0, non-commercial use). Values are modelled on a grid of roughly 11 km, not readings from a monitoring station, so nearby spots often share a value. They may differ from Malaysia's Department of Environment API (IPU) readings.
        • Scale: US AQI categories, not Malaysia's API scale. Particulate AQI uses a 24-hour average, so it can stay high after the air clears. PM2.5 "now" is the current hourly value.
        • Effort zones: heart rate zones use HRmax = 208 − 0.7 × age. Air breathed per minute rises from about 7 litres resting to about 120 in Zone 5, and people switch to mouth breathing at roughly 35 litres a minute. These are population averages, not your body.
        • Dose: PM2.5 (µg/m³) × air breathed. "Estimated to stay in lungs" applies an assumed share (20% at rest, rising to 45% in Zone 5). The direction is supported by research, but the exact percentages are assumptions, not measurements.
        • Cigarettes: popular rule of thumb that about 22 µg/m³ of PM2.5 over 24 hours equals one cigarette, applied to air breathed in. It is a rough comparison, not a medical measure.
        • Advice: the highest suggested zone depends on the US AQI (stricter if you tick the health box). It is combined with current PM2.5: go at ≤ 15 µg/m³ (WHO 24-hour guideline), caution up to 35 (US EPA 24-hour standard), avoid above. The stricter result wins. Running pace is converted to a breathing level by speed, which varies a lot with fitness.
        • Distances: marked "sourced" only where a public source we checked agrees. Others are typical lengths we have not verified.
        • Privacy: your location is used in your browser and sent to Open-Meteo to fetch air quality. Your plan is saved only in this browser. Nothing is sent to us.
